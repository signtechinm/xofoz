# XOFOZ Solution Pages — Design and Image Production Plan

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the phased plan for creating XOFOZ solution pages. These are not service pages. A service page explains a capability XOFOZ provides; a solution page begins with a business problem or desired outcome and shows how XOFOZ assesses, designs, implements, and supports the complete answer.

The existing service pages are used only as a visual-system reference for typography, spacing, colors, cards, particles, gradients, and motion. The solution pages need their own problem-led information architecture.

## 3. Solution-Page Positioning

Each solution page should answer five questions quickly:

1. What business problem is the customer experiencing?
2. What is causing or contributing to that problem?
3. How does XOFOZ assess and design the right answer?
4. Which services and products are combined to deliver the solution?
5. What practical business outcome should the customer expect?

### Service page versus solution page

| Service page | Solution page |
|---|---|
| Starts with a capability | Starts with a business problem or desired outcome |
| Explains what XOFOZ provides | Explains how XOFOZ solves the complete situation |
| Often focuses on one discipline | Can combine several services and products |
| Uses service inclusions | Uses an assessment-to-outcome plan |
| CTA asks for a service quote | CTA asks for an assessment, consultation, or site survey |

## 5. Proposed Solution-Page Architecture

### Section 1 — Problem-led hero

- Eyebrow identifying the customer situation
- Question or outcome-led H1
- Direct answer explaining how XOFOZ solves it
- Primary assessment CTA
- WhatsApp CTA
- Relevant proof points
- Solution-specific hero visual
- Interactive particles used with restraint

Example H1 style: “Is your office Wi-Fi slow, patchy, or unreliable?”

### Section 2 — Recognize the problem

- Observable symptoms
- Business consequences
- Who is affected
- When the problem becomes urgent
- Diagnostic or signal-style visual treatment

### Section 3 — What is usually causing it

- Plain-language explanation of common root causes
- Structured diagnostic categories
- No assumption that every customer needs the same products
- Technical blueprint or system-map graphic

### Section 4 — The complete XOFOZ solution

- Explain the combined solution rather than a single service
- Show which service, product, and implementation layers work together
- Use a system diagram or layered solution graphic where useful
- Link to the relevant supporting service and product pages

### Section 5 — Assessment and design

- Explain what XOFOZ inspects or measures
- Show that specification follows assessment
- Include site survey, audit, discovery, or requirements mapping as applicable
- Use a checklist or annotated blueprint treatment

### Section 6 — Delivery roadmap

Recommended general pattern:

1. Assess
2. Design
3. Supply and configure
4. Implement
5. Test and hand over
6. Support

The exact number of steps can vary by solution, but the underlying journey should remain recognizable.

### Section 7 — What is included

- Concrete implementation scope
- Products or platforms where relevant
- Configuration and integration work
- Testing and documentation
- Staff training and handover
- Ongoing support options

### Section 8 — Expected outcomes

- Translate technical work into business results
- Avoid guaranteed numerical claims unless verified
- Use before-and-after comparisons when credible
- Highlight reliability, visibility, productivity, security, scalability, or operational control

### Section 9 — Why XOFOZ

- Local engineering capability
- Verified certifications and partnerships
- Multi-vendor design approach
- On-site accountability
- Relevant industry experience
- Evidence appropriate to this specific solution

### Section 10 — Industries and environments

- Show where the solution applies
- Adapt examples to hospitality, real estate, construction, logistics, manufacturing, and office environments
- Link only to real industry pages

### Section 11 — Frequently asked questions

- Cost factors
- Timeline
- Compatibility with existing systems
- Disruption during implementation
- Support after handover
- Service-specific objections and decision questions
- Visible answers matched by valid FAQ structured data where eligible

### Section 12 — Related services, products, and solutions

Use three clearly separated relationship types:

- Services used to deliver the solution
- Products or platforms used in the solution
- Adjacent problems the customer may also need to solve

### Section 13 — Consultation CTA

- Restate the problem and desired outcome
- Ask for the information needed for an assessment
- Use a solution-specific form field
- Provide phone, email, WhatsApp, hours, and office information
- Use a strong but uncluttered closing background

## 6. Typography Reference

The typography should inherit the visual scale already established on the homepage and updated service pages.

- Font family: Geist
- Solution hero H1: `clamp(1.95rem, 3.45vw, 3.45rem)`
- Primary section H2: `clamp(1.8rem, 2.65vw, 3rem)`
- Major H3: approximately `1.15rem` to `1.65rem`
- Body copy: approximately `1rem` to `1.13rem`
- Eyebrow and metadata: approximately `0.68rem` to `0.76rem`

Rules:

- One H1 per solution page.
- Problem-led titles must remain readable and balanced.
- Long question headlines should not become oversized on mobile.
- Maintain consistent line height, weight, and spacing with the homepage.
- Preserve semantic H1, H2, and H3 order.

## 7. Background and Graphic System

The solution pages should feel more narrative than the service pages. Background styles will change as the visitor moves from problem to diagnosis, design, implementation, and outcome.

| Narrative stage | Proposed visual direction |
|---|---|
| Problem | Signal interference, diagnostic pulse, warning glow, or fragmented grid |
| Root cause | Blueprint, network map, scan line, or technical annotation |
| Complete solution | Connected system diagram, layered rings, or modular blocks |
| Assessment | Measurement lines, coverage field, survey markers, or checklist geometry |
| Delivery | Directional path, staged nodes, or timeline grid |
| Outcome | Cleaner, brighter, calmer field with reduced visual noise |
| Proof | Focused spotlight or contained evidence panel |
| CTA | Strong directional light or closing gradient |

### Constraints

- Do not use a strong graphic background in every section.
- Alternate expressive sections with quieter reading sections.
- Maintain text contrast in dark and light themes.
- Decorative graphics must remain behind content.
- Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Avoid effects that make all solution pages look identical.
- Use solution-specific graphic motifs when they meaningfully explain the problem.

## 8. Image Strategy

Solution-page imagery should show the customer situation, the engineered response, or the successful environment. It should not simply reuse the service-page hero image by default.

### Image types

#### Hero image

- One primary visual per solution
- Show the environment and problem context
- Preserve negative space for responsive crops
- Avoid generated text and fake interfaces

#### Diagnostic graphic

- Optional supporting visual showing causes, coverage, flow, or system relationships
- Prefer code-native diagrams when clarity matters more than photographic realism

#### Outcome visual

- Optional before/after or completed-environment visual
- Use only where it adds information rather than decoration

### Preliminary visual concepts

- CCTV: commercial property with purposeful camera coverage and subtle coverage overlays
- Office Wi-Fi: modern office floor with visualized access-point coverage zones
- IT troubleshooting: engineer diagnosing an office system with hardware, software, and network layers
- Cloud migration: controlled movement from on-premise infrastructure to Microsoft cloud collaboration
- New-office setup: coordinated office technology installation shown as one integrated environment
- ERP implementation: connected operations across finance, inventory, sales, and management reporting

### Image rules

- Use realistic UAE commercial environments.
- Avoid generic futuristic holograms.
- Keep devices, racks, cameras, cables, and installations technically plausible.
- Do not generate vendor logos, certification marks, or readable interface text.
- Avoid distorted people, equipment, or architecture.
- Keep XOFOZ blue and cyan as controlled accents rather than overwhelming color casts.
- Standardize master exports near the current `1600 × 1000` ratio.
- Export optimized WebP assets and suitable social-preview versions.

## 10. Quality Standards

The solution-page rollout is complete only when:

- The definitive solution inventory is approved.
- Every page begins with a real customer problem or desired outcome.
- Every solution clearly connects the required services and products.
- Typography matches the homepage and service-page visual system.
- Background graphics reinforce the narrative without reducing readability.
- Imagery is specific to the solution rather than a renamed service image.
- Technical diagrams are accurate and accessible.
- All pages work at desktop, tablet, and mobile widths.
- All factual claims are verified.
- All forms and contact actions work.
- Structured data matches visible page content.
- The production build completes without errors.
